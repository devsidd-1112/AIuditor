# User Interviews

## Overview

User interview strategy and findings for AIuditor. Interviews validate assumptions, uncover pain points, and guide product decisions.

---

## Target Users

### Primary: Startup Founders (2-10 person teams)
**Why**: Direct budget control, feel pain of overspending

**Characteristics**:
- Technical or semi-technical
- Using 3-5+ AI tools
- Monthly AI spend: $200-1000
- Budget-conscious
- Time-constrained

---

### Secondary: Engineering Managers (10-50 person teams)
**Why**: Manage team tooling, report to finance

**Characteristics**:
- Responsible for developer productivity
- Evaluating tool ROI
- Justifying spend to leadership
- Managing seat allocations

---

### Tertiary: Operations/Finance Leads
**Why**: Broader SaaS spend management

**Characteristics**:
- Managing multiple software categories
- Quarterly budget reviews
- Procurement responsibilities
- Looking for optimization opportunities

---

## Interview Goals

### Validate Assumptions
1. Do teams actually overspend on AI tools?
2. Are overlapping subscriptions common?
3. Do small teams pay for enterprise features unnecessarily?
4. Is manual auditing too time-consuming?

### Uncover Pain Points
1. How do teams currently track AI spending?
2. What triggers a spending review?
3. What makes it hard to optimize?
4. What would make optimization easier?

### Understand Workflows
1. Who approves new AI tool subscriptions?
2. How often are tools reviewed?
3. What tools are considered "essential"?
4. How are savings decisions made?

---

## Interview Questions

### Opening (5 min)
**Goal**: Build rapport, understand context

1. "Tell me about your role and your team."
2. "How many people are on your team?"
3. "What's your involvement with software/tool purchasing?"

---

### Current State (10 min)
**Goal**: Understand existing AI tool usage

4. "Which AI tools does your team currently use?"
   - Follow-up: "Which plans are you on?"
   - Follow-up: "How many seats/licenses?"

5. "Roughly how much do you spend on AI tools monthly?"
   - Follow-up: "Is that a guess or do you track it?"

6. "How did you end up with this particular set of tools?"
   - Follow-up: "Who decided on each tool?"
   - Follow-up: "Were alternatives considered?"

---

### Pain Points (10 min)
**Goal**: Identify problems and frustrations

7. "When was the last time you reviewed your AI tool spending?"
   - Follow-up: "What triggered that review?"
   - Follow-up: "What did you find?"

8. "Have you ever felt like you might be overpaying for AI tools?"
   - Follow-up: "What made you think that?"
   - Follow-up: "Did you do anything about it?"

9. "Do you think there's overlap between any of your AI tools?"
   - Follow-up: "Which ones?"
   - Follow-up: "Why keep both?"

10. "What makes it hard to optimize AI tool spending?"
    - Follow-up: "Time? Complexity? Uncertainty?"

---

### Decision Making (10 min)
**Goal**: Understand how changes happen

11. "If you wanted to cancel or downgrade an AI tool, what would that process look like?"
    - Follow-up: "Who needs to approve?"
    - Follow-up: "What concerns would come up?"

12. "Have you ever downgraded or cancelled an AI tool?"
    - Follow-up: "What prompted that?"
    - Follow-up: "How did it go?"

13. "What would make you confident about making a change to your AI tool stack?"
    - Follow-up: "Data? Recommendations? Peer validation?"

---

### Solution Validation (10 min)
**Goal**: Test product concept

14. "If there was a tool that analyzed your AI spending and suggested optimizations, would that be useful?"
    - Follow-up: "What would make you trust it?"
    - Follow-up: "What would make you skeptical?"

15. "How much time would you spend on an AI spend audit?"
    - Follow-up: "What if it took 60 seconds?"
    - Follow-up: "What if it took 30 minutes?"

16. "Would you want this to be free or would you pay for it?"
    - Follow-up: "What would make it worth paying for?"

---

### Closing (5 min)
**Goal**: Gather final insights

17. "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about managing AI tool spending, what would it be?"

18. "Is there anything else about AI tools and spending that we haven't talked about?"

19. "Would you be interested in trying an early version of this tool?"

- Y Combinator community
- Indie Hackers
- Startup Slack groups
- Local startup meetups

**Social Media**:
- Twitter/X (tech Twitter)
- LinkedIn (engineering managers)

**Direct Outreach**:
- Personal network
- Founder friends

## Interview Logistics

### Format
- **Video call** (Zoom, Google Meet)
- **Duration**: 30-45 minutes
- **Recording**: Ask permission, use for notes only

### Preparation
- Review their LinkedIn/company
- Check if they've mentioned AI tools publicly
- Prepare specific follow-up questions

### During Interview
- Listen more than talk (80/20 rule)
- Ask "why" and "tell me more"
- Don't pitch the product
- Take notes on exact quotes

### After Interview
- Send thank you email within 24 hours
- Summarize key insights
- Share relevant findings with team
- Follow up on promised access/incentives

---

## Analysis Framework

### Categorize Findings

**Pain Points**:
- Severity (high, medium, low)
- Frequency (how many mentioned it)
- Urgency (how soon needs solving)

**Workflows**:
- Current process
- Bottlenecks
- Workarounds
- Ideal state

**Objections**:
- Trust concerns
- Time concerns
- Value concerns
- Change resistance

**Feature Requests**:
- Must-have
- Nice-to-have
- Out of scope

---

## Validation Criteria

### Strong Product-Market Fit Signals
- Users describe pain vividly
- Users have tried to solve it themselves
- Users would pay for solution
- Users refer others unprompted

### Weak Product-Market Fit Signals
- Users say "that's interesting" but no emotion
- Users haven't tried to solve it
- Users want it free
- Users don't mention to others

## Key Questions to Answer

### Product
- Is the 60-second audit fast enough?
- Are recommendations clear and actionable?
- Do users trust the savings estimates?
- What's missing from the audit?

### Business
- Would users pay for premium features?
- What features justify payment?
- How much would they pay?
- What's the decision process?

### Growth
- How did they hear about us?
- Would they recommend to others?
- What would make them share?
- What communities are they in?

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

### Product Red Flags
- "That's interesting" (not "I need this")
- "I'd use it if it was free" (no willingness to pay)
- "Let me think about it" (no urgency)
- Can't articulate the problem clearly

### Market Red Flags
- Very few people have this problem
- Problem isn't painful enough
- Existing solutions are "good enough"
- Market is too small

## Success Metrics

### Interview Quality
- 80%+ of interviewees describe pain vividly
- 50%+ have tried to solve problem themselves
- 30%+ would pay for solution
- 20%+ refer others

- 80%+ trust the recommendations
- 70%+ would use it regularly
- 50%+ would recommend to others
